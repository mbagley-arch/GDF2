🎮 Game Development: Pre-Production Project

Game Pitch & Game Design Document

Plan your game before you build it!

Project Overview

Your mission: Develop an original video game concept and create a design plan that explains what your game is, why people might want to play it, and what you will need to make it.

⭐In this project, you will create two connected documents: Link to Templates 

  1. Game Pitch Document: Introduce your game idea and explain why it is worth making.
  2. Game Design Document (GDD): Plan the story, characters, mechanics, levels, engine, and other details needed to develop your game.

Important: You are planning your game, not building the entire game yet. Clear explanations, thoughtful planning, and labeled sketches are more important than artistic skill.

Learning Goals

  • Evaluate your game concept, why it should be made, and what resources it will require.
  • Create a game pitch that communicates your idea to others.
  • Develop a Game Design Document that serves as a blueprint for your game.
  • Plan project time, costs, gameplay, story, characters, and levels before development begins.

Part 5: Grading Rubric

Your project will be scored using the criteria below. The maximum score is 100 points.

Performance levels: Excellent = full or nearly full credit; Proficient = meets the requirement; Developing = partially complete or needs more detail; Beginning = missing, unclear, or very incomplete.

Criteria Excellent Proficient Developing Beginning Points
1. Game Concept and Pitch
15 points		 15–14: Clear, original concept with a strong goal, genre, and explanation of the player experience. 13–11: Understandable concept and goal with most important details included. 10–7: Some concept details are unclear or missing. 6–0: Concept is difficult to understand or largely incomplete. ____ /15
2. Characters, Environment, Obstacles, and Platform
10 points		 10–9: All elements are detailed and fit the game concept. 8–7: Most elements are described clearly. 6–5: Several elements need more detail. 4–0: Many required elements are missing or unclear. ____ /10
3. Storyboards and Visual Planning
10 points		 10–9: Storyboards are complete, labeled, and clearly show the sequence of events and gameplay. 8–7: Storyboards communicate the main sequence with minor gaps. 6–5: Some panels or explanations are missing or difficult to follow. 4–0: Storyboards are mostly missing or do not communicate the game sequence. ____ /10
4. Market Research
10 points		 10–9: Researches at least two relevant games, includes sources, and explains how the new game could stand out. 8–7: Researches two games and provides a reasonable comparison. 6–5: Research or comparisons are incomplete or limited. 4–0: Little research is shown or sources are missing. ____ /10
5. Time, Cost, and Feasibility
10 points		 10–9: Realistic time and cost estimates with thoughtful resource planning and a practical backup plan. 8–7: Includes estimates and explains whether the project is achievable. 6–5: Estimates or feasibility explanation lack detail. 4–0: Planning is missing or unrealistic without explanation. ____ /10
6. Story, Narrative, and Dialogue
10 points		 10–9: Clear story structure with meaningful conflict and at least four appropriate dialogue lines or barks. 8–7: Story structure and dialogue are present and understandable. 6–5: Story or dialogue needs development or has missing parts. 4–0: Story structure or dialogue is largely missing. ____ /10
7. Game Design and Mechanics
15 points		 15–14: GDD clearly explains the game engine, gameplay loop, mechanics, controls, goals, and rules. 13–11: Most required design details are present and understandable. 10–7: Several design elements are incomplete or unclear. 6–0: Major parts of the GDD are missing. ____ /15
8. Level and Character Design
10 points		 10–9: At least two level plans and two character designs are clear, labeled, and support gameplay. 8–7: Required plans and sketches are present with adequate explanations. 6–5: Some plans, labels, or character details are missing. 4–0: Level or character planning is mostly incomplete. ____ /10
9. Reflection and Organization
10 points		 10–9: Reflection shows thoughtful evaluation; submission is complete, organized, and easy to follow. 8–7: Reflection and organization meet expectations with minor omissions. 6–5: Reflection is brief or the submission is difficult to follow in places. 4–0: Reflection is missing or the submission is substantially incomplete. ____ /10
